1. Reasoning & Approach

How I'm thinking about this

You asked for two things:

  1. Subscription/bill tracking — see what regular payments are coming up
  2. Fortnightly spare money — income minus groceries minus subscriptions for your pay cycle

Plus a "nice to have": the app should suggest things that could be bills or subscriptions based on popular names or recurrence in your data.

Here's my reasoning on the best way to deliver this:

Why subscriptions first, not full budgeting

Your app already has a spend pipeline (Woolworths PDF, BankSync, NetBank CSV) and a monthly grocery budget. Subscriptions are the smallest unit of work that delivers the "what's due this fortnight" view. Full budgeting (categories, envelopes, account reconciliation) would be a much larger project with different data models — better to validate the simple version first.

Why a simple table, not a recurrence engine

Subscriptions don't need a complex "next occurrence" calculation engine. A next_due date + frequency is enough — the app computes whether each subscription is due in the current fortnight. This keeps the schema trivial and the logic testable.

Why suggest from purchase data

Your purchases table already stores every transaction with store, order_date, product_name, and line_total. If you buy something regularly from the same merchant at similar amounts, that's a signal. BankSync transactions go further — they include Netflix, Spotify, insurance, utilities. The app can surface these as "you might want to track this as a subscription" without any external API.

Why fortnightly, not monthly

Your existing budget_status() in services/budget.py is monthly-only. You get paid fortnightly, so the fortnight is the natural budgeting period. The grocery estimate from the shopping list already covers a plan period (which is typically 1–2 weeks), so it slots in cleanly.

3. Pain Points & Gaps

Pain Point 1: Non-grocery transactions are discarded

BankSync and NetBank imports only keep transactions matching grocery merchant patterns (Woolworths, Coles, ALDI, etc.). Everything else — Netflix, Spotify, rent, utilities, insurance — is classified as "unclassified" and thrown away. This is the single biggest data gap: the raw material for subscription suggestions already flows through the app but is discarded at the door.

Pain Point 2: No way to track recurring non-grocery expenses

Even if you wanted to manually add "Netflix $18.99/monthly" today, there's nowhere to put it. The purchases table is for actual transactions, not forward-looking commitments. There's no concept of "this much money will leave my account on this date."

Pain Point 3: Monthly budget doesn't match pay cycle

budget_status() computes monthly pacing (spent vs. target, projected, tone). But you get paid fortnightly and want to know "after this fortnight's bills, what's spare?" The monthly view is too coarse — a big bill early in the month makes the whole month look bad even if the fortnight is fine.

Pain Point 4: No "what's due" view

The dashboard shows groceries, meal plan, pantry, and spend — but nothing about upcoming bills. You have to remember that rent is due on the 1st, Netflix on the 15th, etc.

Opportunity: Purchase data already contains recurrence signals

Your purchases table has store, order_date, product_name, and line_total for every transaction. BankSync transactions add description (merchant name) and amount. A simple heuristic — "same description, similar amount, 3+ times, regular interval" — can surface candidates without any external API or ML.

4.2 Fortnightly Summary

The fortnightly summary page shows:

Fortnight: Mon 12 Oct – Sun 25 Oct
─────────────────────────────────────────
  Income (fortnightly)           $2,400.00
  Groceries (planned)             -$185.00
  Subscriptions due               -$127.50
    Netflix                       -$18.99
    Rent                         -$100.00
    Spotify                         -$8.51
  ─────────────────────────────────────────
  Spare                         $2,087.50

Calculation logic (pure function in services/budget.py):

  1. Determine fortnight start/end (Mon–Sun, 14 days). Current fortnight = the one containing today.
  2. Sum subscriptions where next_due falls within the fortnight, OR frequency implies a payment is due (monthly bills whose due date is in range, weekly × 2, etc.).
  3. Get grocery estimate from the current meal plan's shopping list (already computed by shopping_list_generator.py).
  4. Subtract both from a configured fortnightly income (new setting: fortnightly_income).

4.3 Subscription Suggestions from Purchase Data

Two detection strategies, both pure functions over purchase dicts:

Strategy A: Recurrence detection (from purchases)

Scan purchases for patterns:

  • Same store (or product_name prefix) appearing 3+ times
  • Similar line_total (within ±10%)
  • Regular interval (roughly weekly, fortnightly, or monthly)

Surface as: "You've paid Woolworths $85.30 about weekly — is this a regular expense?" (No — it's groceries. But the signal is there.)

Strategy B: BankSync unclassified transactions

BankSync already pulls all transactions but only keeps grocery ones. The "unclassified" list is currently discarded. Instead, keep it in memory during sync and surface descriptions that look like bills:

  • Known merchant patterns (Netflix, Spotify, AGL, Origin Energy, etc.)
  • Merchant names that repeat across syncs
  • Round amounts on regular dates

Surface as: "These merchants appear regularly in your bank feed — add them as subscriptions?"

Recommendation: Start with Strategy B (BankSync unclassified) — it's higher signal, lower effort, and the data is already flowing. Strategy A can be a later enhancement.

4.4 User Workflow

  1. Add a subscription — manual form (name, amount, frequency, next_due, category)
  2. See upcoming — fortnightly summary shows what's due
  3. Get suggestions — app surfaces potential subscriptions from bank data
  4. Confirm/dismiss — one click to add a suggested subscription, or dismiss it
  5. Track spare — fortnightly summary shows income − groceries − subscriptions = spare

6. Recommendations

Do this now

  1. Subscriptions table + CRUD — the foundation. Small, self-contained, immediately useful.
  2. Fortnightly summary — the "what's spare" view. This is the core value proposition.
  3. BankSync unclassified suggestions — the data is already flowing; just keep it and surface it.

Don't do this yet

  1. Full budgeting — categories, envelopes, account reconciliation. Different data model, much bigger project.
  2. Recurrence detection from purchases — interesting but noisy (groceries will dominate). BankSync unclassified is cleaner.
  3. Automatic "mark as paid" tracking — keep it manual for now. Auto-detection adds complexity.

Technical notes

  • The subscriptions table goes in SCHEMA (not MIGRATIONS) — new tables are created automatically by init_db().
  • Fortnightly period = Monday to Sunday (14 days). The current fortnight contains today's date.
  • The existing budget_status() pattern (monthly pacing with tone) is the template for fortnightly_summary().
  • All new routes follow the existing blueprint pattern: POST-only for state changes, CSRF protected, @login_required.
  • The suggestion feature should be opt-in — surface suggestions but require explicit "accept" before creating anything.
